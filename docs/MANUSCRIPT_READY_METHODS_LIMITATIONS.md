# Manuscript-ready text — evidence-bounded version (English)

This wording is suitable as a **draft for coauthor review**, not a complete manuscript, and must be reconciled with the complete original local preprocessing scripts and source clinical metadata before submission. The three primary hypotheses were not significant; avoid presenting the work as mechanistic proof.

## Methods — Source cohorts and unit of replication

We analyzed previously published colorectal cancer single-cell RNA-sequencing data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO): GSE132465 (SMC cohort; 23 Korean patients, 23 primary tumor specimens and 10 matched normal mucosa specimens; 63,689 author-QC-retained cells) and GSE144735 (KUL3 cohort; six Belgian patients, each sampled at tumor core, tumor border and matched normal mucosa; 27,414 author-QC-retained cells). Both cohorts originate from the same published study (Lee et al., *Nature Genetics*, 2020); therefore, KUL3 was treated as a distinct-patient cohort for descriptive cross-cohort assessment, rather than an independently published external validation cohort. Patient identifiers, not cells or within-patient tissue regions, were the independent analysis units. Tumor core and border were summarized separately or combined within each patient, not treated as independent patients.

## Methods — Original and secondary QC, annotation and scoring

The original study applied UMI-, feature- and mitochondrial-content-based quality control, harmonized initial cell identities using multiCCA and RCA, and removed discordant identities and suspected high-feature-count multiplets. We used author-QC-retained processed UMI count matrices and recorded residual contamination and donor-imbalance concerns rather than assuming the data were free of doublets. Candidate macrophage, fibroblast and CD8 populations were defined using lineage and original annotation evidence independently of the gene-program scores. In KUL3 the user-approved primary analytical subsets comprised 1,745 macrophage candidates from myeloid clusters 0/1/2/5, 4,230 fibroblast candidates from stromal clusters 0/2/3/4/9/11/12, and 1,264 author-annotated CD8-positive T cells from clusters 2/4/10 after per-cell subtype gating. All four prespecified signatures were frozen between cohorts (29 program–gene pairs: SPP1_TAM, 7; FAP_CAF, 7; CD8_Dysfunction, 8; CD8_Cytotoxicity, 7) and scored using UCell with maxRank = 1,500.

## Methods — Statistical comparisons

For the primary SMC tumor-only assessment, patient-level mean program scores were compared using Spearman rank correlations among 11 complete-case patients with at least 20 eligible cells in each relevant compartment. Permutation p-values were adjusted for the three prespecified comparisons using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. In KUL3, we descriptively compared each patient's mean program score between tumor and matched normal mucosa, restricting each paired contrast to at least 20 eligible cells on both sides. Patient-level tumor–normal differences were summarized by the median across matched eligible patients. Core, border and pooled core-plus-border are alternative views of the same six patients and were not interpreted as independent replication sets. We evaluated sensitivity to cross-lineage exclusion, omission of high-risk clusters and broader author-labeled CD8 inclusion; these analyses did not select the primary mask post hoc.

## Results — concise validated wording

In SMC, none of the three prespecified cross-program associations was statistically significant after multiplicity correction (SPP1_TAM–FAP_CAF Spearman ρ = 0.400, BH-adjusted permutation p = 0.669; SPP1_TAM–CD8_Dysfunction ρ = 0.082, adjusted p = 0.817; FAP_CAF–CD8_Dysfunction ρ = −0.182, adjusted p = 0.817; n = 11). In KUL3, relative to matched normal mucosa, the combined core-plus-border tumor compartment showed higher SPP1_TAM scores in 4 of 4 evaluable patients (median paired difference +0.344 UCell units) and higher FAP_CAF scores in 6 of 6 (median +0.228). CD8 dysfunction showed higher scores in 3 of 3 evaluable patients (median +0.027), but CD8 representation and selection sensitivity constrained interpretation. These paired tumor–normal contrasts do not assess cross-program coupling.

## Limitations — publication-ready wording

Several limitations constrain inference. First, the SMC cross-program analysis included only 11 complete-case patients and none of the prespecified correlations was significant after correction for multiple testing. Second, the KUL3 cohort, although comprising different patients, was part of the same original publication, limiting claims of external-study replication and exposing findings to study-wide analytical and cohort-design dependencies. Only two KUL3 patients had at least 20 eligible cells for all three targeted compartments in tumor core, and only three had such coverage after combining core and border; consequently, no confirmatory three-program correlation was attempted. Third, donor-dominated clusters and co-detection of cross-lineage transcripts limit certainty of subset assignments. Prior source-author QC does not exclude residual doublets, ambient RNA or cell-state ambiguity, and neither independent computational doublet correction nor orthogonal cell-identity validation was performed in this secondary analysis. High-risk cluster exclusion reduced SPP1 eligible matched patient numbers from four to two in the combined tumor contrast. Fourth, matched contrasts involved varying subsets of evaluable patients across programs, and UCell score deltas are not interchangeable effect sizes across independent signatures. Finally, no spatial colocalization, ligand–receptor interaction validation, functional perturbation, clinical outcome prediction or causal mechanism was established. We interpret the findings as descriptive tumor enrichment of candidate SPP1- and FAP-associated programs, not confirmation of a coupled SPP1–FAP–CD8 dysfunction axis.
